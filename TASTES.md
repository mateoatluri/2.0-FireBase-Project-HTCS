# Web Experience Tastes

## Core orientation

I enjoy web experiences that help me learn, create, and solve problems. I am drawn to interfaces that feel intelligent and intentional: they should let me explore, but should not make essential functions difficult to find.

## Experiences that stand out

One memorable experience was a homepage showing a horse formed from many black dots. Scrolling animated the horse moving within that dot pattern. What appeals to me about this kind of experience is the combination of a recognizable subject and an abstract visual system, plus the discovery that an ordinary action like scrolling can reveal motion and meaning. I like when interaction is not merely a control, but part of the visual idea.

## Interaction preferences

- I enjoy small hidden details and mechanisms that reward curiosity.
- I like interfaces that are fast, seamless, and slightly minimalist.
- I prefer exploratory interfaces, provided I can still reliably access what I need through dropdowns, boxes, or other clear navigation.
- I especially value search that understands related or approximate language instead of requiring an exact phrase.
- Interaction should feel responsive and purposeful rather than busy for its own sake.

## Visual taste

I prefer a restrained visual system with one or two carefully chosen accent colors. I like thoughtful color palettes, minimalism, and occasional objects or motifs—such as flowers—that add character without overwhelming the page. I dislike excessive color, too many simultaneous moving elements, visual overstatement, and lag.

## Attention and pacing

Websites should respect my attention. I prefer calm, efficient experiences that allow focus and do not constantly interrupt, animate, or demand engagement. Delight is welcome when it is subtle, discoverable, and connected to the design rather than added as noise.

## Content interests

I enjoy reading the Financial Times and The Wall Street Journal to learn about current events and financial news. This suggests I value credible, information-rich content and interfaces that support understanding, scanning, and finding relevant material quickly.

## Things I reject

I dislike websites that are over the top, overloaded with colors or motion, or technically inefficient. I also dislike the increasingly generic visual language of AI-assisted websites when they all look the same. Distinctiveness matters to me, but it should come from thoughtful interaction, content, and details—not from decoration alone.

## What makes my taste distinctive

My ideal web experience balances two qualities that can seem opposed: it gives me room to explore and discover, while remaining fast, legible, and dependable. I am especially interested in the moment when a simple interface reveals a deeper mechanism—like scrolling transforming a dotted horse into a moving image. I want design to reward attention without demanding it, and to make learning or problem-solving feel slightly playful.
